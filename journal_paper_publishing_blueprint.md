## 1. First constraint: UGC-CARE status in 2026

Do not present the target as an "active UGC-CARE approved journal" without verification. UGC discontinued the UGC-CARE journal listing system in 2025. The UGC public notice dated 16 July 2025 states that the list of 1474 UGC-CARE journals as on 10 February 2025 is now a reference resource only, and inclusion or exclusion does not imply validation or endorsement by UGC.

This matters because this is a first publication. Do not rely on WhatsApp lists, paid publication agents, "guaranteed Scopus" offers, or websites that say "UGC CARE 2026 approved". For JNU and supervisor review, the safe wording is:

> The target journal is peer-reviewed, indexed in Scopus or Web of Science, and appears in the historical UGC-CARE reference list or satisfies UGC's current suggestive parameters for peer-reviewed journals.

For this paper, the safer publication strategy is:

1. Prefer Scopus or Web of Science indexed journals in economics or applied economics.
2. Prefer Indian economics journals with established institutions behind them.
3. Use the historical UGC-CARE list only as supporting evidence, not as the sole basis for choosing a journal.
4. Before submission, ask JNU or the supervisor which policy they will apply: Scopus/WoS, the UGC historical reference list, or the new UGC suggestive parameters.
5. Prefer journals with no mandatory submission fee or publication fee.
6. Submit through the journal's official website only.

Best practical interpretation for Prof. Shakti Kumar's review: choose a journal that is visibly peer-reviewed, indexed, non-predatory, and respected in Indian economics. Do not chase fast publication.

## 3. Beginner publishing workflow

Follow this exact sequence.

1. Prepare the professor draft.
   - Use the 10-page structure in this blueprint.
   - Put "Under the supervision of Prof. Shakti Kumar" on the professor version only.
   - Do not call the paper accepted, submitted, UGC approved, or Scopus accepted.

2. Get supervisor clearance.
   - Ask whether Prof. Shakti Kumar should be co-author, acknowledged supervisor, or only internal supervisor.
   - Do not put his name as journal co-author unless he agrees.

3. Choose one journal only.
   - Do not submit to multiple journals at the same time.
   - Simultaneous submission is an ethics violation and can get the paper rejected or blacklisted.

4. Make two files.
   - Title page: author names, affiliation, email, acknowledgement, funding, conflict of interest, data availability.
   - Anonymous manuscript: no author name, no supervisor name, no acknowledgements, no self-identifying file name.

5. Check the journal format.
   - Abstract word limit.
   - Total word limit.
   - Keywords and JEL codes.
   - Reference style.
   - Table and figure placement.
   - Declarations required.
   - Current indexing status on the journal page and, if possible, Scopus Sources or Web of Science Master Journal List.

6. Submit through official portal only.
   - For SAGE journals, use the official SAGE/PeerReview/SageTrack link from the journal page.
   - For Springer journals, use the official Editorial Manager link from Springer.
   - Never send files to a random Gmail unless the official journal page itself gives that email.

7. After submission.
   - Wait for editorial screening.
   - If desk rejected, revise and submit to the next journal.
   - Do not argue emotionally with editors.
   - Do not pay any surprise fee. If a charge appears, stop and verify with the official editorial office.

## 8. Abstract for the 10-page paper

Use this for Margin and Indian Economic Journal. It is under 150 words.

> This paper studies how global oil-price shocks pass through India's domestic price system. Instead of estimating only the effect of oil prices on headline CPI, it follows the shock across wholesale, retail fuel, fuel-sensitive consumer, and headline consumer price layers. Monthly log-difference models are estimated using short-run asymmetric ADL specifications with Newey-West HAC inference. The results show a layered attenuation pattern. Retail petrol prices respond strongly to Brent shocks, while WPI Fuel and Power responds strongly to rupee oil shocks. CPI Fuel and Light shows a smaller but significant bridge response. Headline WPI shows statistically significant but modest pass-through. Headline CPI shows weak positive pass-through that is not statistically significant at conventional levels. The evidence does not support strong short-run asymmetry, except for a marginal retail petrol result. The findings suggest that oil shocks remain visible in fuel layers but lose force in broad consumer inflation.

For Springer journals, expand this to 180 to 220 words by adding sample periods and diagnostic language.

## 9. Core thesis of the paper

One-sentence thesis:

> Oil-price shocks in India are transmitted strongly to retail fuel and fuel-sensitive wholesale prices, but the signal becomes much weaker in headline consumer inflation.

Use this contribution statement:

> The paper contributes by treating oil-price pass-through as a layered price-system problem rather than as a single elasticity between crude oil and headline CPI.

Do not say:

- "Oil prices determine inflation in India."
- "The results prove deregulation caused higher pass-through."
- "WPI transmits oil shocks to CPI in a single causal chain."
- "Headline CPI pass-through is zero."
- "Asymmetry is the central finding."

Say instead:

- "The estimates suggest a layered pattern of attenuation."
- "Headline CPI pass-through is weak and statistically insignificant."
- "The post-2010 wholesale estimates are consistent with stronger pass-through under more market-linked fuel pricing, but they are not a clean causal estimate of deregulation."
- "The models are reduced-form projections."

### 3. Data and variables

Target length: 550 to 700 words.

Use only active data and output folders:

- `models/data`
- `models/wpi`
- `models/cpi`

Do not use stale root-level model outputs.

Define:

`oil_INR = Brent_USD * INR_per_USD`

`Delta x_t = 100 * [ln(x_t) - ln(x_{t-1})]`

Positive and negative shocks:

`Delta x_t^+ = max(Delta x_t, 0)`

`Delta x_t^- = min(Delta x_t, 0)`

Important interpretation:

- CPT+ is the cumulative response to positive shock terms.
- CPT- is the sum of coefficients on negative shock terms.
- Since the negative shock variable is non-positive, do not describe CPT- as an identical positive shock.

### 4. Methodology

Target length: 650 to 800 words.

Use this model equation:

`Delta y_t = alpha + sum_i phi_i Delta y_{t-i} + sum_j beta_j^+ Delta x_{t-j}^+ + sum_j beta_j^- Delta x_{t-j}^- + gamma'Z_t + mu_m + epsilon_t`

Explain in words:

- `Delta y_t` is monthly inflation in the relevant price index.
- `Delta x_t` is the shock variable for that layer.
- WPI models use rupee oil shocks.
- Retail petrol uses Brent shocks.
- CPI Fuel and Light uses PPAC petrol shocks.
- Headline CPI uses rupee oil shocks.
- `Z_t` contains controls where relevant, including activity controls and policy-period dummies.
- `mu_m` represents month fixed effects.

Use this model-role table only if space allows. If page limit is tight, merge it with Table 1.

| Model | Dependent variable | Shock variable | Main role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Headline WPI | Headline WPI inflation | Rupee oil shock | Main wholesale result |
| WPI Fuel and Power | WPI Fuel and Power inflation | Rupee oil shock | Wholesale fuel mechanism |
| PPAC retail petrol | Delhi petrol inflation | Brent shock | Direct retail fuel mechanism |
| CPI Fuel and Light | CPI Fuel and Light inflation | PPAC petrol shock | Consumer fuel bridge |
| Headline CPI | Headline CPI inflation | Rupee oil shock | Consumer endpoint |

Inference:

- Main inference uses Newey-West HAC standard errors.
- Report tests for CPT+ = 0, CPT- = 0, and CPT+ = CPT-.
- Bootstrap symmetry checks are robustness evidence, not separate models.
- Diagnostics include serial correlation, functional form, and stability checks.

Do not include NARDL, bounds tests, or error-correction language. This paper is short-run ADL only.

### 7. Discussion and conclusion

Target length: 450 to 600 words.

Discussion:

- The result is not that oil is unimportant.
- The result is that headline CPI absorbs and dilutes oil shocks because the index is broad.
- WPI, retail petrol, and CPI reveal different parts of the same inflation process.
- A policy reader should monitor fuel-sensitive layers instead of relying only on headline CPI.

Conclusion structure:

1. Restate research question.
2. Summarise layered ranking.
3. State that asymmetry is not the main finding.
4. State the post-2010 split carefully.
5. Close with a precise sentence.

Good final sentence:

> The main lesson is that oil shocks do not disappear in India, but they lose force as they move from fuel prices to broad consumer inflation.

## 17. Reviewer-risk controls

Risk 1: "This is not causal."

Response in paper:

> The estimates are reduced-form short-run projections and should not be read as structural causal effects.

Risk 2: "Different samples weaken the chain."

Response in paper:

> The layer-specific samples reflect data availability. The common-sample attenuation check supports the same qualitative pattern.

Risk 3: "Why Delhi petrol?"

Response in paper:

> PPAC Delhi retail petrol provides a consistent long monthly retail series and is used as a direct retail fuel-price channel. The result should be read as channel evidence rather than a complete national retail fuel measure.

Risk 4: "Why no NARDL or cointegration?"

Response in paper:

> The paper studies short-run monthly pass-through in log differences. The objective is not to estimate a long-run equilibrium relation, so ADL models are more consistent with the claim being made.

Risk 5: "WPI Fuel and Power fails RESET."

Response in paper:

> The WPI Fuel and Power model is reported as mechanism evidence with a functional-form caveat. The main WPI model passes the diagnostic gate.

Risk 6: "Asymmetry is weak."

Response in paper:

> Correct. The paper does not make asymmetry the main result. The central finding is layered attenuation.

Risk 7: "Headline CPI coefficient is insignificant."

Response in paper:

> Correct. That is part of the result. The paper does not claim significant headline CPI pass-through. It claims that the oil signal becomes weak at the headline CPI layer.

Risk 8: "Post-2010 is not clean deregulation."

Response in paper:

> Correct. The paper treats the split as institutional evidence consistent with stronger pass-through under more market-linked pricing, not as a causal estimate of deregulation.
