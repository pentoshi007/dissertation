# Synopsis

## Title

State-dependent pass-through of rupee oil shocks to India's WPI Fuel and Power inflation, 1994-2026

## Background and research gap

India is a large crude-oil importer, so international oil-price movements matter for domestic prices through both Brent prices and the rupee-dollar exchange rate. Existing Indian studies show that oil shocks can affect inflation and output, but many papers either use broad headline inflation, shorter samples, or single-equation pass-through models. This fallback study uses a cleaner long monthly sample and focuses on the fuel-sensitive wholesale layer where the economic channel is direct.

The research gap is not whether oil matters in a general sense. The gap is whether pass-through to India's WPI Fuel and Power index is stronger after domestic fuel pricing became more market-linked, and whether a state-dependent local-projection design gives a more defensible model than a single linear ADL equation.

## Research question

How do rupee-denominated Brent crude shocks transmit to India's WPI Fuel and Power inflation over 0 to 12 months, and is the response stronger after the 2010 fuel-pricing reform period?

## Objectives

1. Build a consistent monthly WPI Fuel and Power dataset from official OEA WPI workbooks, chained to 2011-12 = 100.
2. Construct a rupee oil shock as `100 * Delta ln(Brent_USD * INR_per_USD)`.
3. Estimate cumulative local-projection responses over horizons 0 to 12 months.
4. Test whether the response differs after the 2010 petrol-pricing reform period.
5. Report post-2014 diesel deregulation and high-oil-volatility checks as robustness, not as the main claim.

## Hypotheses

H1: A positive rupee oil shock is associated with a positive cumulative response of WPI Fuel and Power inflation.

H2: Pass-through is stronger in the post-2010 fuel-pricing reform period than in the pre-2010 period.

H3: Post-2014 diesel deregulation evidence should be treated as robustness because the preliminary interaction is positive but not consistently significant at the six-month horizon.

H4: Positive and negative shock asymmetry is secondary and should not be the paper's main contribution.

## Data

The main sample is monthly, from April 1994 to March 2026. After using a full year of dynamic controls, the main estimation sample is May 1995 to March 2026, giving 30.92 years.

Main sources:

- WPI Fuel and Power: official Office of the Economic Adviser WPI workbooks, chained to 2011-12 = 100.
- Brent crude oil price: World Bank Pink Sheet monthly Brent series.
- INR/USD exchange rate: FRED `EXINUS_latest` source file already used in the project.

Processed files are generated by `new-model/R/run_all.R`:

- `new-model/data/processed/wpi_fuel_chained_2011.csv`
- `new-model/data/processed/model_dataset.csv`

## Methodology

The study uses local projections following Jorda (2005). For each horizon `h = 0, ..., 12`, the cumulative response is estimated as:

```text
100 * [ln(WPI_Fuel_{t+h}) - ln(WPI_Fuel_{t-1})]
  = alpha_h + beta_h * Delta oil_INR_t
  + controls + month fixed effects + error_{t+h}
```

The state-dependent version adds:

```text
Delta oil_INR_t * Post2010_t
```

Controls include six lags of WPI Fuel and Power inflation, six lags of the rupee oil shock, six lags of INR/USD changes, month fixed effects, and a COVID shock dummy. All inference uses Newey-West HAC standard errors because local-projection residuals can be serially correlated, especially at longer horizons.

## Preliminary findings from the implemented pipeline

The all-sample response is positive and statistically significant at all horizons.

- Horizon 6: estimate = 0.2963, p < 0.001.
- Horizon 12: estimate = 0.3461, p < 0.001.

The post-2010 interaction supports the state-dependent claim.

- Horizon 6 post-2010 additional response = 0.1972, p = 0.0220.

The post-2014 robustness is positive but weaker.

- Horizon 6 post-2014 additional response = 0.0988, p = 0.2809.

The horizon-0 diagnostics show why the state-dependent model is preferred. The simple all-sample LP is rejected by the HAC RESET benchmark, while the post-2010 state-dependent LP passes the functional-form and stability checks. Serial correlation is handled by Newey-West HAC inference, and a GLS AR(1) robustness check gives a Ljung-Box lag-12 p-value of 0.9974 for normalized residuals.

## Expected contribution

The contribution is a long-sample, fuel-layer pass-through study for India. It avoids overclaiming about headline CPI and instead studies the price layer where oil should be most visible. The novelty is the state-dependent local-projection design, which connects India's fuel-pricing reforms with the timing and strength of wholesale fuel pass-through.

## Limitations

This is not a structural global oil-market decomposition. It does not separately identify oil supply, global demand, and oil-specific demand shocks in the Kilian (2009) sense. It also does not claim that the 2010 reform is a clean natural experiment. The post-2010 result should be interpreted as state-dependent evidence consistent with more market-linked fuel pricing, not as a fully causal deregulation estimate.
