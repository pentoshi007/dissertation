# Journal Paper Publishing Blueprint

## Paper title

State-dependent oil-price pass-through to wholesale fuel inflation in India: Local-projection evidence, 1994-2026

## Publication positioning

This should be submitted as a narrow applied time-series paper, not as a broad dissertation summary. The journal-safe contribution is:

> A long-sample local-projection estimate of rupee oil pass-through to India's WPI Fuel and Power index, with state dependence around the post-2010 fuel-pricing reform period.

Do not promise that the paper cannot be rejected. The aim is to remove avoidable rejection triggers: mixed timelines, unclear data provenance, overclaimed causality, unreported diagnostics, copied prose, and predatory-journal targeting.

## Target journal order

### Target 1: The Indian Economic Journal

Best first submission target.

Why it fits:

- Indian economics scope with empirical policy analysis.
- SAGE journal and COPE member.
- Official guidelines state there are no fees payable to submit or publish under the normal route.
- Word format accepted.
- APA reference style.
- British spelling required.
- Abstract should be 150 to 200 words.
- 4 to 6 keywords and JEL codes required.

Fit strategy:

- Keep the paper compact.
- Put literature inside the introduction rather than creating a long separate literature chapter.
- Use three main tables and three figures.
- Keep post-2014 and asymmetry checks as robustness.

### Target 2: Margin: The Journal of Applied Economic Research

Good second target if the supervisor wants a more applied-economics outlet.

Why it fits:

- SAGE/NCAER applied economics journal.
- Official guidelines state no fees payable to submit or publish under the normal route.
- Manuscripts should not ordinarily exceed 8,000 words including references, tables, and figures.
- Abstract not more than 150 words.
- 4 to 6 keywords and JEL codes required.
- Strictly anonymized peer review.

Fit strategy:

- Frame the result as applied inflation and pricing-policy evidence.
- Keep the quantitative method clear but not over-technical.
- Make state dependence the novelty.

## Abstract draft

This paper studies the pass-through of global oil shocks to India's wholesale fuel inflation. The shock is measured as the monthly log change in Brent crude prices multiplied by the INR/USD exchange rate, and the dependent variable is India's WPI Fuel and Power index chained to 2011-12 = 100. Using monthly data from 1994 to 2026, the paper estimates cumulative local projections over horizons from 0 to 12 months with Newey-West HAC inference and twelve monthly dynamic controls. The results show a positive and statistically significant all-sample response. At six months, a 1 per cent rupee oil shock is associated with a 0.2963 per cent cumulative response in WPI Fuel and Power; at twelve months, the estimate is 0.3461. The response is stronger after the 2010 fuel-pricing reform period, with an additional six-month response of 0.1972. The evidence is interpreted as state-dependent wholesale fuel pass-through, not as a general claim about headline consumer inflation.

## Submission ethics checklist

- Submit to only one journal at a time.
- Use an anonymized manuscript for peer review.
- Put author name, affiliation, email, acknowledgements, funding, conflict of interest, and data availability on a separate title page.
- Do not list the supervisor as co-author unless he explicitly agrees.
- Do not use publication agents or guaranteed-acceptance services.
- Do not choose optional open access unless funding is confirmed.
- Include a data availability statement explaining that the analysis uses public OEA, World Bank, and FRED data, with scripts provided for replication.
- Mention AI assistance only if the target journal or supervisor requires it. Do not list AI tools as authors.
