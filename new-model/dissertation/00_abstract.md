# Abstract

This dissertation examines the pass-through of rupee-denominated crude oil price shocks to India's Wholesale Price Index (WPI) Fuel and Power component over the period 1994 to 2026. The study constructs a chained monthly WPI Fuel and Power index from three official Office of the Economic Adviser base-year series and combines it with World Bank Brent crude oil prices and the INR/USD exchange rate to form a rupee oil price measure. Using Jordà (2005) local projections with Newey–West heteroskedasticity and autocorrelation consistent standard errors, the analysis estimates cumulative impulse responses at horizons from zero to twelve months. The main estimation sample runs from May 1995 to March 2026, spanning 30.92 years after accounting for twelve monthly dynamic controls. The all-sample six-month cumulative response of WPI Fuel and Power inflation to a one per cent rupee oil shock is 0.2963 per cent and is statistically significant at the one per cent level. A state-dependent specification interacting the shock with a post-2010 fuel-pricing reform indicator adds 0.1972 percentage points at the six-month horizon, with a p-value of 0.0220. The state-dependent specification passes functional-form and stability diagnostics, and a GLS AR(1) robustness check confirms the serial-correlation treatment. The findings suggest that fuel-price deregulation strengthened the wholesale fuel price channel, implying that inflation management should track the rupee oil price rather than Brent alone.
