# Conclusion

This dissertation set out to answer whether rupee-denominated Brent crude oil shocks transmit to India's wholesale fuel inflation, and whether the transmission strengthened after the fuel-pricing reforms of 2010. Using monthly data from May 1995 to March 2026 and Jordà (2005) local projections with Newey–West HAC inference, the analysis provides clear evidence on both questions.

Rupee oil shocks have a positive and statistically significant cumulative association with WPI Fuel and Power inflation at every horizon from zero to twelve months. At the six-month benchmark, a one per cent rupee oil shock is associated with a 0.2963 per cent cumulative response, significant at the one per cent level. At twelve months, the estimate is 0.3461. The response profile suggests that most of the pass-through is completed within four to six months, consistent with the periodic adjustment of domestic fuel prices to global benchmarks.

The state-dependent specification reveals that the pass-through is substantially stronger in the post-2010 period. The post-2010 interaction adds 0.1972 percentage points to the six-month response, with a p-value of 0.0220. The total post-2010 six-month response is 0.3937, approximately double the pre-2010 response of 0.1965. The interaction remains significant through most horizons, and the state-dependent specification passes functional-form, stability, and serial-correlation robustness diagnostics.

The post-2014 diesel deregulation interaction is positive in sign but lacks statistical precision at the six-month horizon and is therefore treated as supporting robustness rather than as a headline claim. Similarly, the asymmetry and high-volatility exercises do not overturn the main finding and are reported as secondary checks.

The policy implication should be stated cautiously. The evidence suggests that fuel-price deregulation, by removing the administered-price buffer between global oil markets and domestic wholesale prices, has made India's wholesale fuel prices more responsive to rupee oil shocks. For inflation management purposes, policymakers and forecasters should monitor the rupee oil price—the product of Brent and the exchange rate—rather than tracking the dollar-denominated oil price alone. Exchange-rate movements amplify or dampen the pass-through of global oil shocks, and ignoring this channel would understate the effective oil-price impulse facing the Indian economy.

This study does not claim that oil shocks mechanically determine all Indian inflation. The WPI Fuel and Power component is one layer of a complex price system, and the pass-through from wholesale fuel prices to headline consumer inflation involves additional channels that are beyond the scope of this analysis. What the evidence does support is a narrower and more defensible claim: that rupee oil shocks transmit clearly and significantly to India's wholesale fuel price index, and that this transmission became stronger after the institutional shift from administered to market-linked fuel pricing.
